Bbar 9

How it plays

Right hand

Raise it, drop it: that downbeat starts a one-bar count-in. Every ictus after that sets the tempo, 60 to 160 BPM.

Left palm

Its height is the dynamics, pp at your lap to ff at eye level. Louder floods more of the wall with colour.

Point & pinch

Point a hand at a section's wall and pinch to cue it. Hold the pinch 0.6 s to solo, again to mute.

Fist

Either hand. The orchestra stops on the spot, the walls go dark, and one bell rings.

Palm up opens the wrist menu: pieces, Listen (the orchestra plays itself, your hands paint light), Ghost (replay your last take), calm light, one-hand mode, latency calibration.

Cbar 17

The real room decides

  1. 17

    Where each section sits. Detected planes carry no labels, so Jangdan reads orientation and height: left wall for strings, right for winds, front wall or ceiling for brass, the table for the janggu.

  2. 18

    Where the light falls. Each wash is drawn on the plane's own polygon, so the colour stops at the real wall's edge.

  3. 19

    Where the sound comes from. An HRTF panner sits at each surface's centroid.

  4. 20

    How it rings. The room's bounding box gives a volume; Sabine's formula gives a decay time; the impulse response is generated in code. A small office is dry. A loft is wet.

  5. 21

    When a wall is missing, a paper banner hangs where it would be, and it says so.

Ebar 33

Honest notes

  • Built and tested in the IWER emulator with emulated hands (Quest 3 and Meta VR Glasses profiles). The numbers are in TESTING.md.
  • Not yet verified on a headset: Quest Browser audio latency (the calibration tap exists for it), real hand-tracking velocity noise, and 72 fps.
  • Quest 3 has no eye tracking: cues use the hand ray. Only on Meta VR Glasses does "look at the strings and pinch" use real eye gaze.
